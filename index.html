What it does

Nine things that add up to a better morning.

1Set it up

Say what kind of morning you want.

Pick what fits: Stay informed, Commuter, Start the day with music, or My channels. They combine. Or describe your morning in a sentence and EarlyWire sets it up. Then it builds your first morning while you watch.

  • Change anything later, one setting at a time or in a sentence.
  • No account, and nothing to sign in to.
2Home

One morning, top to bottom, with an end.

Home starts with your Deep Dives, each saying what's new since the last issue. Then the news, your video lists and music. At the bottom it says "That's your morning," so you know you're done.

  • Pull down to refresh the news, video lists and music.
  • Tap Play all and a whole list opens in the YouTube app, with your own account.
3Deep Dives

Follow a subject in depth.

A company, a competitor, a hobby, a field of research. Give a Deep Dive its topics and EarlyWire gathers the news, groups the repeats and lays each story out with its outlet, headline, summary and picture. Each issue leads with what's new since the last one, not the same pile again.

  • Scan, Brief or Deep: headlines only, a short summary, or a longer read.
  • As many Deep Dives as you like, each with its own name and schedule.
4Go deeper

More on any one story, from its sources.

Tap Go deeper on an item and EarlyWire reads the article and other outlets covering it, then explains the story at more length: what happened, the background, who is involved and what comes next. It names the outlet for what only one reported, and says what the sources don't cover.

  • With your own AI key it writes one explanation. Without one it shows more of the articles' own words.
  • The sources are always listed, one tap away.
5The big picture

Once a week, what it all added up to.

For a subject you follow every day, turn on the weekly big picture. On the day you choose, EarlyWire pulls the week's issues into a few themes, each linked back to the stories behind it.

  • Written from issues already built, so nothing is searched again.
  • Without an AI key it lists each topic's main stories of the week.
6Past issues

Every issue, kept.

Each Deep Dive keeps its past issues on your device, with what each one added. Go back to last Tuesday in two taps.

  • The newest 52 issues are kept for each Deep Dive.
7Listen

Have it read to you. Hands free.

Switch a Deep Dive from Read to Listen and it reads what's new. Say or press More, Next or Repeat from your headphones, your lock screen or your voice.

  • Start it with Siri: "Listen to my Deep Dives in EarlyWire."
  • A Lock Screen and Control Center control starts it too.
8Video lists

Your own rows of videos.

Pick channels you like, follow a YouTube playlist, or start from an idea such as Science Fun, Cooking, Hero Animals or Sports Highlights. Each list becomes a row on Home, with the newest videos each morning.

  • Set a time limit on any list, such as "stop at 15 minutes."
  • News and music work the same way: the day's top stories from the outlets you choose, and new songs from your artists.
9Schedule

Say when it should be ready.

Pick a "Ready by" time and the days, once, and everything follows it. Give any Deep Dive or your video lists their own time when they need one. Ready means ready by then, even if iOS builds a few hours earlier.

  • Every day, weekdays, every other week or once a month.
  • Home Screen and Lock Screen widgets show what was built and when the next build is due.

Private by design

There is no EarlyWire server to trust.

The app does the work on your device. We never see your subjects, your channels or what you ask.

  • No account.Open it and start. There is nothing to sign up for.
  • No ads, no analytics, no tracking.Nothing in the app reports back to us.
  • Your data stays on your device.Settings, issues and keys live in the app's private storage and your Keychain. With iCloud on, your settings and latest builds also sync between your own devices through Apple's iCloud, never through us.
  • AI that stays on your device by default.Apple's on-device model helps set things up, and nothing in the everyday app asks you to pick an AI. Claude, ChatGPT or Gemini is optional, with your own key, and only when you use it. Step-by-step guides make the key in a few minutes. Keys stay on your device.

Questions

Straight answers.

What does it cost?

EarlyWire is free to try for 7 days. After that, a single one-time purchase of $4.99 keeps it on your iPhone, iPad and Mac. No subscription, and nothing renews. If you choose to add your own Claude key, Anthropic bills that usage to your own account; that is optional.

Do I need an account?

No. There is no EarlyWire account. You only sign in to Google if you turn on emailing a Deep Dive's issue to yourself or your team, and then only to send mail you asked for.

Does it download or copy YouTube videos?

No. It reads public information about videos and channels to choose what to show. When you tap Play all, the list opens in the YouTube app or on youtube.com, with your own account and any Premium benefits you have.

Do my iPhone and Mac share settings?

Yes. When you are signed in to the same iCloud account, your Deep Dives, channels, rules and schedules stay the same on every device, and so do the latest builds, so a device that sees one doesn't build it again. It all goes through Apple's iCloud and not through us. Each device keeps its own Google sign-in. Your Claude key stays on the device unless you choose "Use this key on all my devices" under AI, which shares it through iCloud Keychain, encrypted end to end by Apple. You can turn syncing off in Settings.

When does it build my morning?

iOS decides when background work runs, which is usually overnight while your device charges. Whatever is due is also built the moment you open the app, and each part shows when it was last built. On a Mac it builds on schedule while the app is open or at login.

Do I need Apple Intelligence or an AI key?

No. Without either, EarlyWire picks and groups stories by rules, and shows the article's own first sentence under each headline. Apple's on-device model, on devices that have it, helps with setup. Claude, ChatGPT or Gemini, with your own key, improves choices, summaries and suggestions. Gemini is free to start.

Can I trust the summaries?

Every item names its outlet and links to the original. Without an AI key, each item shows the article's own opening sentence rather than a rewrite. With your own Claude, ChatGPT or Gemini key, the AI writes the summaries and is told to use only what the sources say; Go deeper and the weekly big picture say who wrote them and list their sources. An AI can still get things wrong, so the articles are the record.

Which devices does it run on?

iPhone and iPad with iOS or iPadOS 26.1 or later, and Mac with macOS 26 or later.

Is EarlyWire affiliated with YouTube, Google, Anthropic or OpenAI?

No. It is an independent app. YouTube, Google, Gemini, Claude and ChatGPT are trademarks of their owners.
