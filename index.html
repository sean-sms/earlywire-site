What it does

Seven small things that add up to a better morning.

1Set it up

Describe your morning in your own words.

"I follow the Braves, local Atlanta news and upbeat 90s country." EarlyWire fills in the sports, news, music and a written brief for you. Or tick a template and go.

  • Templates combine: Sports Enthusiast, Breaking News, Top 10 Music Videos.
  • Change anything later, one setting at a time or in a sentence.
2Watch

Sports, news and music, in one tap.

Each morning's picks are ranked by your rules and trimmed to the time you have. Tap Play all and the whole list opens in the YouTube app, with your own account and Premium benefits.

  • 45-minute sports, 30-minute music, as short or long as you like.
  • EarlyWire only reads public YouTube information. It never changes your account.
3Read

Written briefs on anything you follow.

Competitors, a hobby, a team, a recipe box. Give a brief its keywords and EarlyWire gathers the news, groups the repeats and shows the article's own opening sentence with a link to read more.

  • Make as many briefs as you like, each with its own name and schedule.
  • Every day, every other week, or once a month.
4Listen

Have it read to you. Hands free.

Switch a brief from Read to Listen and the words follow along as they are spoken. Say or press More, Next or Repeat from your headphones, your lock screen or your voice.

  • Start it with Siri: "Listen to my briefs in EarlyWire."
  • A Lock Screen and Control Center control starts it too.
5Describe

Start a brief from a sentence.

Say what you want to follow and EarlyWire suggests a name, topics and keywords. You review them before anything is saved. It uses Apple's on-device model, or Claude if you add your own key.

6Adjust

Change a setting by saying what you'd change.

"Make it a bit shorter and drop the Shorts." EarlyWire proposes exact changes, such as 45 to 30 minutes. Each one has a switch, and nothing is saved until you approve.

  • Every proposed change is checked in code against what you actually typed.
7Glance

Widgets that tell you it's ready.

Home Screen and Lock Screen widgets show what was built, how long each part is and when the next build is due, so you know before you open anything.

Private by design

There is no EarlyWire server to trust.

The app does the work on your device. We never see your channels, your briefs or what you ask.

  • No account.Open it and start. There is nothing to sign up for.
  • No ads, no analytics, no tracking.Nothing in the app reports back to us.
  • Your data stays on your device.Settings, briefs and keys live in the app's private storage and your Keychain. Delete the app and they go.
  • AI that stays on your device by default.Apple's on-device model helps set things up. Claude is optional, with your own key, and only when you use it.

Questions

Straight answers.

Is it free?

Yes. EarlyWire is free and has no in-app purchases. If you choose to add your own Claude key, Anthropic bills usage to your own account there. That is optional.

Do I need an account?

No. There is no EarlyWire account. You only sign in to Google if you turn on emailing a finished brief to yourself or your team, and then only to send mail you asked for.

Does it download or copy YouTube videos?

No. It reads public information about videos and channels to choose what to show. When you tap Play all, the list opens in the YouTube app or on youtube.com, with your own account and any Premium benefits you have.

When does it build my morning?

iOS decides when background work runs, which is usually overnight while your device charges. Whatever is due is also built the moment you open the app, and each part shows when it was last built. On a Mac it builds on schedule while the app is open or at login.

Do I need Apple Intelligence or Claude?

No. Without either, EarlyWire picks and groups stories by rules, and shows the article's own first sentence under each headline. Apple's on-device model, on devices that have it, helps with setup. Claude, with your own key, improves choices and suggestions.

Can I trust the summaries?

Each item shows the article's own opening sentence with a link to the original, rather than a rewrite. Where a model writes a summary, the app checks it against the source and drops it if it can't be backed up.

Which devices does it run on?

iPhone and iPad with iOS or iPadOS 26.1 or later, and Mac with macOS 26 or later.

Is EarlyWire affiliated with YouTube, Google or Anthropic?

No. It is an independent app. YouTube, Google and Claude are trademarks of their owners.
